Paying Only the Minimum on $8,000? The 23-Year Credit Card Math

The short answer

In our simulation, paying only the minimum on $8,000 at 22.36% APR takes 278 months (about 23 years) and costs about $21,831. Simply keeping the first minimum payment fixed at $229 clears it in 57 months.

Key numbers

  • Average rate on card accounts charged interest: 22.36% (Federal Reserve G.19, Aug 2026 data).
  • Interest on $8,000 at that rate: about $4.90 a day, roughly $149 a month.
  • Minimum only: $13,831 of interest. A fixed $300 a month: 38 months, about $11,163 total.
  • Your statement's minimum payment warning shows how long the minimum takes on your own card.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to pay off a credit card paying only the minimum?

It depends on your balance, APR and your issuer's minimum formula. In our example ($8,000, 22.36% APR, 1% of balance plus interest with a $25 floor), it takes 278 months, about 23 years.

Why does the minimum payment take so long?

As the balance falls, the minimum falls too, so less goes to the debt each month. In our example about 65% of each minimum payment is interest.

How do I avoid credit card interest on purchases?

Most cards have a grace period: paying the full statement balance by the due date usually means no interest on purchases, according to the CFPB.

Assumptions

$8,000 balance, 22.36% APR, monthly interest = APR ÷ 12 (simplified; many issuers compound daily), example minimum = 1% of balance + interest with a $25 floor, no new purchases, no fees. Jordan is a fictional example.
